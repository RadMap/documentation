Errata

Updated 2026-11-13.

Faults found in released versions of RadMap. Each entry says which versions it affects, whether it can change results and under which conditions, and the version that fixes it. Every PDF report names the RadMap version that produced it, so you can tell whether a result needs review.

This page is updated whenever a fault is found, independently of releases. To report a problem, use Report a problem.

Versions affected

An x marks a version with the fault. Results says whether the fault can change a simulation's results: yes, no, or tiny -- by too little to matter, though a digit of a result can change. The entry says under which conditions, and by how much. Versions before 1.1.0 were pre-releases and are not listed.

Older stands for the versions released more than 2 years ago, 1.1.0: an x there means one of them has the fault, and the entry says which.

D-0001: Sources in a moved parent, or rotated tube segments, sampled in the wrong place

  • Found: 2026-10-22, by the V&V case R6-Sampling, written to check where a source's decays happen
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: for a source in a solid that sits inside a moved solid, at any depth, and for a source in a rotated tube segment. Either the run stopped at its first decay, with an error or a crash, or it completed with decays in only part of the source's volume, the part the sampling box happened to overlap. The dose rates of such a run describe a source of the right activity in the wrong place: too high near the part that had decays and too low elsewhere, by an amount that depends on the geometry. Sources in solids placed directly in the world volume, and full tubes, were not affected.
  • Users told: on this errata page, and in the release notes of 2.0.0
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

RadMap tells Geant4 to sample decay positions in a box around the source solid and keep those inside it. The box's centre was the solid's position relative to its parent, not in the world, and its offset from the solid's origin was not turned with the solid. So for a source in a moved parent, or a rotated segment whose bounding box is off its origin, the box sat away from the solid. Results of such projects made with 1.1.0 should be recalculated with 2.0.0.

D-0002: Sampling box of a curved source fitted to its drawing mesh, slightly too small

  • Found: 2026-10-22, while designing the check that refuses sources too thin to sample
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: negligible. For sources in tubes and spheres, thin slivers at the curved surfaces, at most about 0.2 % of the radius deep and together about 2 parts in 10 000 of the source's volume, never had decays. The activity was not lost but spread over the rest of the source, a few millimetres away, so dose rates changed by far less than 0.1 %, well within the statistical uncertainty of any run. Sources in boxes were not affected. No recalculation is needed.
  • Users told: not needed; listed on this errata page
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

RadMap tells Geant4 to sample decay positions in a box enclosing the source solid and keep those inside it. The box was fitted to the corners of the mesh used to draw the solid, and a curved surface bulges out beyond the mesh between its corners, so the box stopped short of the solid there. The box is now computed exactly from the solid's dimensions.

D-0003: Effective dose used ICRP 116's coefficients without their 2015 corrections

  • Found: 2026-10-24, while checking RadMap's coefficient tables against the publications for the V&V report
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: effective dose from photons, in every irradiation geometry. At the energies radionuclides emit, up to 10 MeV, RadMap's coefficients were up to 1.7 % high in LLAT, 1.2 % high in RLAT, 1.2 % low in PA, 0.9 % low in ROT, and within 0.6 % in ISO and AP, depending on the energy; a dose rate differs by at most as much. Air kerma and H*(10) are not affected. Only an effective dose result used within about 2 % of a limit needs recalculating.
  • Users told: not yet
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

ICRP corrected 199 of the photon effective dose coefficients in Table A.1 of Publication 116 in 2015 (Corrigenda, Annals of the ICRP 44(1)). RadMap's tables were transcribed from the original 2010 table and carried the uncorrected values, although the corrections were published before RadMap's first release. RadMap now uses the corrected table, and its data source reads ICRP 116 (2010, corrected 2015).

D-0004: Isotopes offered whose ground state has no decay data emitted nothing

  • Found: 2026-10-27, by a new test that runs every offered isotope's decay data through RadMap's parser
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: for a source of Ag-94, Bi-198, Lu-154, Nb-85, Po-193, Ra-201, Re-194, Se-82 or Tl-186 only. The source emitted no radiation, and every dose map read zero, without a warning. Of these, only Bi-198 (half-life 11.6 minutes) is a likely choice: Se-82 is practically stable and the others have half-lives under 12 seconds. Results of a project with such a source should be recalculated with another isotope.
  • Users told: on this errata page, and in the release notes of 2.0.0
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

The isotope list offered every isotope Geant4 has a decay file for. For some, the file describes only an excited state of the nucleus, and Geant4 can then not decay the ground state a source starts in. In the decay data 1.1.0 used (Geant4's RadioactiveDecay 4.3.2) that held for the nine isotopes above; in the data 2.0.0 uses it holds for 39. The isotope list now leaves out every isotope whose ground state has no decay data, and a project that holds one is refused before the simulation starts, with a message naming the source.

D-0005: Deep dose maps read the radiation where it entered them, not their mean

  • Found: 2026-10-28, while writing a V&V case for dose maps turned with a corner towards a source
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: for dose maps deep along the direction the radiation comes from, where it changes across them: close to a source, or at the edge of a shield's shadow. A box facing a small source read high by about half its depth divided by its distance: 5 % for a 10 cm cube at 1 m, 15 % for a 30 cm cube. Turned with a corner towards the source, the same cubes read 0.2 % and 2.4 % low. Plates facing the source changed little: 0.1 % for a plate 2 mm thick at 1 m. A dose map enclosing a source, or part of one, did not count the photons emitted inside it and read low, by an amount depending on how much of the source it held. Results from dose maps deeper than a few per cent of their distance from a source, or enclosing one, should be recalculated.
  • Users told: on this errata page, and in the release notes of 2.0.0
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

A dose map is meant to read the dose rate averaged over its volume. It counted the photons entering it through any face, each divided by the area of the box seen from its direction. That is the mean over the box only where the radiation is the same throughout it, as for a thin plate. Where the radiation changes across a deep box, the count follows the faces the photons enter by, which depend on how the box is turned, so the same box in the same place read differently in different orientations. RadMap now adds up the length of every photon's path inside the box and divides by its volume, which is the mean over the volume by definition, in any orientation and wherever the photons come from.

D-0006: Rotated dose maps simulated turned the opposite way

  • Found: 2026-10-28, by the V&V case O-Co60-Orientation, written to check dose maps turned towards a source
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: for a dose map with a rotation of its own, or attached to a rotated solid. The map was in the right place but turned the opposite way from the one RadMap showed: about one axis, by the angle with its sign reversed; about several, by the inverse rotation. The map then covered a different region from the one drawn, and for a deep map near a source its reading also changed with its orientation (D-0005). The effect is negligible for a map small compared with its distance from sources and shield edges, and grows with its size: a 120 × 240 × 480 mm box turned about two axes, 600 mm from a source, covered a region where the mean dose rate is 4.5 % higher. Results from large rotated dose maps near sources or shield edges should be recalculated.
  • Users told: on this errata page, and in the release notes of 2.0.0
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

RadMap hands Geant4 a dose map's rotation as three angles, from which Geant4 builds a rotation matrix for the map's box. Geant4 uses that matrix as the rotation of the box's frame, which turns the box itself by the inverse. Solids reach Geant4 by another route that has always allowed for this; dose maps did not. RadMap now hands Geant4 the inverse rotation for dose maps too.

D-0007: Dose maps turned close to a quarter turn about Y simulated up to 1° off

  • Found: 2026-10-28, by a new unit test requiring a dose map to turn in Geant4 as a solid with the same rotation does
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: negligible. For a dose map whose overall rotation, its own combined with its solids', came within about 1.15° of a quarter turn about Y -- for instance a map turned 89.5° about Y -- the map was simulated as if turned the full quarter turn, tilted by up to 1.15° from the orientation shown. Turned exactly 90°, it was simulated as shown. A tilt of 1.15° changes the region a map covers so little that a 30 cm cube reads within 0.02 %, and a 120 × 240 × 480 mm box 600 mm from a source within 0.4 %. No recalculation is needed.
  • Users told: not needed; listed on this errata page
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

RadMap hands Geant4 a dose map's rotation as three angles about X, Y and Z. When the second angle came within about 1.15° of ±90°, the conversion set it to exactly ±90°; on the negative side it also turned the map half a turn about its own Z axis, which maps a box onto itself. The angles are now computed exactly at every orientation.

D-0008: A full turn of a tube or sphere entered in radians left a slit

  • Found: 2026-10-28, while reviewing how tubes and spheres check their angles
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: for a tube or sphere whose sweep was meant as a full turn but entered in radians, or a sphere meant to reach a pole in radians. The fields keep three decimals, so a full turn could not be entered exactly: 6.283 rad, the nearest, is 0.011° short of it, and the solid was simulated with a slit that wide running through it, 0.19 mm at a radius of 1 m; 6.284 rad was refused. Photons heading into the slit crossed the solid unattenuated. The effect depends on the geometry: it is largest for a thick shield, at points behind it in line with the slit. Angles entered in degrees were not affected. Results from shields whose sweep was entered in radians should be recalculated.
  • Users told: on this errata page, and in the release notes of 2.0.0
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

RadMap took a tube's or sphere's sweep exactly as entered. A sweep within 0.05° of a full turn, or of the pole for a sphere's theta, is now taken as exactly that, so 6.283 and 6.284 rad both give a closed solid.

The version after 2.0.0 simulates a sweep exactly as entered again, so that what is simulated is always what was typed: a full turn is entered in degrees, and 6.283 rad leaves its 0.011° slit, as the user guide explains. A project saved by 2.0 opens with such a sweep as the full turn it was simulated as.

D-0009: A material with its density in kg/cm³ could not be simulated

  • Found: 2026-10-29, while reviewing how materials reach Geant4
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: none. A simulation with a material whose density was given in kg/cm³ stopped before its first decay, with Geant4's error that the unit kg/cm3 does not exist, so no result was produced. Materials in g/cm³ or mg/cm³ were not affected.
  • Users told: not needed; listed on this errata page
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

RadMap handed Geant4 each density in the unit the user chose, by the unit's name, and Geant4 knows g/cm³ and mg/cm³ but not kg/cm³. RadMap now hands Geant4 every density in g/cm³, converted. It also no longer offers kg/cm³, in which every real material is a small fraction -- osmium, the densest, is 0.0226 kg/cm³ -- and reads a library holding one as g/cm³, the value a thousand times larger.

D-0010: A solid's material could change to one whose name differs only in case

  • Found: 2026-10-29, while reviewing the user interface's event handling
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: only in a project whose material library holds two materials whose names differ only in upper and lower case, such as Lead and LEAD, which RadMap's own library does not. A solid given the second of them was shown with the first, and editing anything else about that solid -- its name, colour, wireframe or axes -- gave it the first: it was then simulated in a material the user had not chosen. Results from projects whose library holds such a pair should be checked: each solid's material is shown correctly from 2.0.0, and a solid showing the wrong one should be recalculated.
  • Users told: on this errata page, and in the release notes of 2.0.0
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

The properties panel looked up a solid's material in its list of materials ignoring case, while material names are case-sensitive, and wrote the material it found back to the solid with the solid's next edit. It now looks the material up by its exact name.

D-0011: The 3D view's arrow keys could act as if Ctrl or Shift were still held

  • Found: 2026-10-29, while reviewing the 3D view's input handling; confirmed by hand
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: none. Only how the view moves with the keyboard was affected; no project, file or result.
  • Users told: not needed; listed on this errata page
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

The view kept its own record of which of Shift, Ctrl and the arrow keys were held, and cleared a key only when it saw the key released. A key released while another window had the keyboard stayed held in that record: after Ctrl+S, Ctrl+L or another shortcut that opens a dialog from the view, the arrow keys tilted the view instead of moving it, until Ctrl or Shift was pressed again. The view now reads Shift and Ctrl from each key press, and forgets the arrow keys when it loses the keyboard.

D-0012: A name in a non-Latin script prints as question marks in the PDF report

  • Found: 2026-10-30, while reviewing the report classes
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: none. The numbers are right, but which dose map or source a row belongs to may not be readable, and two names can print the same.
  • Users told: not needed; listed on this errata page
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0, in part: the report warns; the characters still print as question marks

The report is set in Helvetica, one of the PDF standard fonts, which covers Western European letters only. Any other character in a project, dose map or source name, such as Greek or Cyrillic, is printed as a question mark, though RadMap accepts it as a name and shows it correctly everywhere else. The report still supports only these characters, as a deliberate limit, but RadMap now names the affected text when it creates a report.

D-0013: Full chain on one source also applies to the other sources

  • Found: 2026-10-31, while reviewing the simulation classes
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: for a project with Full chain on for one source and off for another whose daughter is radioactive in its ground state. The other source's daughters decay too, so its dose rate is too high, by an amount that depends on the isotopes. Measured: 100 MBq of Te-132 with Full chain off beside 100 MBq of Cs-137 with it on gave 3.3 times the sum of the two run alone, because Te-132's daughter I-132 emits far more photons. Projects where every source has the same setting are not affected.
  • Users told: on this errata page, and in the release notes of 2.0.0
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

The setting is applied when a decay starts, and a flag recording it was set but never cleared. After the first decay of a source with Full chain on, every later decay on that thread followed its chain, whichever source it came from. Results of such projects made with 1.1.0 or a development build should be recalculated.

D-0014: A source in a solid with "0x" in its name is not confined to the solid

  • Found: 2026-10-31, while reviewing the simulation classes
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: for a source in a solid whose name contains "0x", such as Plate100x50. Decays are placed anywhere in the box around the solid: also inside its child solids, and outside a tube or sphere. Dose rates can be too high or too low, depending on the geometry. Measured: a source in a 5 mm air shell around a lead core gave a dose rate 3.9 times lower with a name containing "0x", as most decays landed in the lead. A box with no children is not affected. Nothing warned of it.
  • Users told: on this errata page, and in the release notes of 2.0.0
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

Reading the geometry, Geant4 cuts every name at "0x", where its own file format puts a pointer. RadMap then asked for the volume by its full name, Geant4 did not find it, reported that only in its log, and sampled the box unconfined. Results of such projects made with 1.1.0 or a development build should be recalculated.

D-0015: Materials named alike up to "0x" are simulated as one material

  • Found: 2026-10-31, while reviewing the classes that save and load a project
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: for a project using two materials, two elements or two isotopes whose names are the same up to a "0x", such as Wall0xA and Wall0xB. Both are simulated as whichever was defined first, so dose rates can be wrong by any amount, in either direction. Measured: a vacuum shield beside a lead material of such a name gave a dose rate 187 times too low. Nothing warned of it. One such name on its own, with no other that matches it, is not affected.
  • Users told: on this errata page, and in the release notes of 2.0.0
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

Geant4 cuts these names at "0x" where it defines them as well as where it uses them, whatever it is told about stripping names. The fix for D-0014 covered solids only. Results of such projects made with 1.1.0 or a development build should be recalculated.

D-0016: A run stops when two elements share an isotope

  • Found: 2026-10-31, while reviewing the classes that save and load a project
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: none. The run stops with "Referenced isotope ... was not found!"
  • Users told: not needed
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

The geometry file listed the second element above the isotope it shares with the first, and Geant4 reads the file from the top.

D-0017: Save Volume writes a file that loses solids when names repeat

  • Found: 2026-10-31, while reviewing the classes that save and load a project
  • Versions affected: none. The fault was made and found during the development of 2.0.0, and fixed before its release; 1.1.0 checked the names.
  • Effect on results: none directly: the solids missing after Load Volume can be seen.
  • Users told: not needed
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

Save Volume did not check names, as a project save does: the check was lost when Save Volume was rewritten for 2.0.0. Solids are found by name on loading, so of two with the same name one took the other's place.

D-0018: An isotope's Z could be edited to one its element cannot hold

  • Found: 2026-11-01, while reviewing the API classes
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: none. The element's isotopes no longer shared one Z, and the run stops with a Geant4 error.
  • Users told: not needed
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

Editing an element checked that its isotopes share one Z, but editing one of those isotopes did not.

D-0019: A source in the world volume is not confined to it

  • Found: 2026-11-01, by hand while checking a source in the world volume
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: for a source in the world volume of a project with solids in the world. Decays are placed anywhere in the world: also inside the solids, not only around them. Dose rates can be too high or too low, depending on the geometry. Measured: a source in the 100 mm of air around a lead cube gave a dose rate 2.1 times lower, as most decays landed in the lead. A world with no solids in it is not affected. Nothing warned of it.
  • Users told: on this errata page, and in the release notes of 2.0.0
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

Geant4 places the world volume itself, under a name of its own, where every other solid is placed by a line of the geometry file under the name RadMap gives it. RadMap asked for the world by the usual name, Geant4 did not find it, reported that only in its log, and sampled the world unconfined. Results of such projects made with 1.1.0 should be recalculated.

D-0020: A field marked invalid in the material editor stayed red for the next entry

  • Found: 2026-11-02, by hand while checking the material editor
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: none. The red marks only the field; a valid value in it is applied as usual.
  • Users told: not needed
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

Choosing another entry, or another kind of entry, filled the fields without clearing the mark an invalid value had left on them, so the new entry's valid value showed red.

D-0021: Argon and potassium had each other's atomic weight in the default library

  • Found: 2026-09-14, while correcting the default material library (commit 11230f7), before this log was started; logged 2026-11-03
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0
  • Effect on results: not measured. Argon was given 39.0983 instead of 39.948, and potassium the reverse, so each element's number of atoms per gram was about 2 % off: argon in AIR, and potassium in the twelve materials holding it, among them tissue-equivalent ones and POTASSIUM_IODIDE. The attenuation by these elements changes by as much; a material's density is not affected.
  • Users told: on this errata page, and in the release notes of 2.0.0
  • Fixed in: 2.0.0

The two entries were swapped in the library RadMap ships, from which every new project takes its own copy. Existing projects keep the library they were saved with: correct the two atomic weights in the project's material library, or reset it to the default library.

D-0022: The user guide recommended ROT, not AP, as the body orientation for one source

  • Found: 2026-11-06, while reviewing the advice against ICRP 116's coefficients
  • Versions affected: 2.0.0
  • Effect on results: RadMap calculates every orientation correctly; the advice chose the wrong one. A dose map set to Effective dose (ROT) for a person facing one source reads lower than Effective dose (AP): by 15 % for Co-60, 20 % for Cs-137 and about 36 % for Am-241.
  • Users told: on this errata page
  • Fixed in: the next release, commit 41812aa

The user guide's Choosing what the dose map scores section called ROT "generally the most meaningful choice for a single-source scenario". For photons from 10 keV to 6 MeV, which covers the gamma lines of nearly all radionuclides, AP gives the highest effective dose of the six orientations in ICRP 116, so it is the cautious choice for a person facing the source or standing in an unknown way. ROT suits a person who moves about and turns relative to the source; ISO, sources all around. Results calculated with ROT on the guide's advice should be compared with AP.

D-0023: A simulation kept running after RadMap ended abnormally

  • Found: 2026-11-09, while making an internal error close RadMap with a message
  • Versions affected: 2.0.0
  • Effect on results: none. The abandoned run's results were never read or saved.
  • Users told: on this errata page
  • Fixed in: the next release, commit 22b0680

DoseAlgorithm, the process that runs a simulation, was ended by RadMap when a run was cancelled or RadMap closed normally, but not when RadMap itself ended abnormally -- a crash, or ended from Task Manager. The simulation then ran on to its end in the background, using the processor, with nothing to read its results. RadMap now starts DoseAlgorithm in a Windows job that ends it with RadMap, however RadMap ends.

D-0024: A pasted value was changed instead of refused

  • Found: 2026-11-10, while examining the hazard that a project holds other than the user entered
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0 to 2.0.0
  • Effect on results: only for a number pasted into a field with more decimals than the field shows, three, after which another value in the same window was edited. The pasted number was then replaced by its rounding to three decimals: a position or dimension moved by at most 0.0005 of its unit, 0.5 mm in metres, and a number below 0.0005 became 0. An angle sweep pasted as 6.2831853 rad became 6.283 rad, leaving a slit in a full turn. A number typed, never pasted, was not affected. A name cut short changed no result.
  • Users told: on this errata page
  • Fixed in: the next release, commit a772b3a

A field refused typing that its limits did not allow, but not pasting. A number with more decimals than its field shows was taken whole, and shown rounded once the cursor left the field; an edit of any other value in the same window -- even retyping one as it was -- read every field again, and wrote the rounded number into the project. A pasted name longer than a name may be was cut to fit, without a word. A paste now goes into a field whole, if typing could have entered it, or not at all.

D-0025: A simulation refused for several problems showed only one as the error

  • Found: 2026-11-11, by a user starting a simulation of a project whose world had neither dimensions nor a material
  • Versions affected: 2.0.0
  • Effect on results: none. The simulation was refused, as it should be.
  • Users told: on this errata page
  • Fixed in: the next release, commit 9713410

When a simulation was started for a project with more than one problem, such as a solid with neither dimensions nor a material, the error named only the first problem and listed the others under Details, as if they were of another kind. The error now says that the project cannot be saved, and lists every problem under Details.

D-0026: An isotope's atomic mass was rounded when the isotope was saved

  • Found: 2026-11-11, while deciding how many decimals a number field must hold
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0 to 2.0.0
  • Effect on results: negligible. The atomic mass changed by less than 5·10⁻¹⁰ u in 2.0.0, and by less than 10⁻⁶ u in 1.1.0, far below anything a dose rate depends on.
  • Users told: on this errata page
  • Fixed in: the next release, commit ea3a5a3

The material editor shows an atomic mass with at most nine decimals, but thirteen isotopes in the default material library -- H1, D2, T3, He3, He4, C13, N14, N15, O16, Ne20, Na23, Si28 and Ar40 -- have masses with ten or eleven. Saving such an isotope in the editor, for any change or none, wrote its mass as the editor showed it, rounded to nine decimals: H1's 1.00782503207 u became 1.007825032 u. RadMap 1.1.0 held the mass as a single-precision number and rounded it to about seven digits. The editor now shows eleven decimals, as many as the library's most precise value has, and RadMap refuses a value with more, so a field never shows a value rounded.

D-0027: A dose map reaching outside the world volume read too low

  • Found: 2026-11-12, by a user noticing that such a project was simulated without a word; confirmed by a run
  • Versions affected: 1.1.0 to 2.0.0
  • Effect on results: a dose map lying partly outside the world read too low, by the fraction of its volume outside: half outside, it read half the dose rate in the part inside. A dose map wholly outside read zero. Maps inside the world were not affected.
  • Users told: on this errata page
  • Fixed in: the next release, commit 575599a

A dose map reads the dose rate averaged over its volume. Nothing is simulated outside the world volume, so the part of a map outside it scored nothing, and its reading was averaged over the whole volume all the same. RadMap allowed such a map and simulated it without a warning. In a run with a Co-60 source, a box half outside the world read half what a box of its inside half alone read, to within 0.1 %.

D-0028: The material editor could save a material mix as a mix of elements

  • Found: 2026-11-13, by a user switching a new material mix to an element mix in the material editor
  • Versions affected: 2.0.0
  • Effect on results: a material saved this way was made of the elements named like its materials, in the same mass fractions. Where each of those materials is the pure element of its name, as every such material in the default library is, the composition was the same. Otherwise it changed as far as those materials differ from those elements, and so did every dose rate computed with it. A mix holding a material that no element is named like was refused, as it should be.
  • Users told: on this errata page
  • Fixed in: the next release, commit c68d378

RadMap 2.0.0 lets a material be a mix of other materials; 1.1.0 has no such mixes. In the material editor, switching a saved material mix -- one just applied, or chosen from the list -- to Element mix emptied the grid and filled it again with the material's own components, now listed as elements, and Apply saved them as elements. Switching the mix now always starts an empty grid, and the saved components come back only under the mix they were saved as.
